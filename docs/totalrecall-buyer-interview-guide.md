# TotalRecall buyer interview guide

## Purpose

Learn whether a specific business workflow has enough painful, contextual, and permitted follow-up work for a memoryful calling agent. This is a discovery interview, not a sales call and not a request to process real contact data.

## Recruitment screener

Recruit people who:

- own or closely supervise a recurring follow-up process;
- can discuss a recent lost, stalled, or delayed relationship without sharing personal customer data;
- use phone calls as part of the workflow today; and
- can describe the CRM, spreadsheet, inbox, or notes where context lives.

Avoid recruiting solely from people enthusiastic about AI. Include at least one operator who dislikes automated calling so the study surfaces real objections.

## Session structure - 45 minutes

### Warm-up - 5 minutes

1. What is your role, and which relationships or conversations are you responsible for moving forward?
2. Which kinds of calls are important to your work but easy to delay?

### Current workflow - 10 minutes

1. Walk me through the most recent time someone needed a follow-up call.
2. What triggered the call? Who should have made it? What happened instead?
3. Where did the caller look for context before calling?
4. Who decides whether a call is appropriate, and what happens when the answer is unclear?
5. What system is supposed to track next steps? What actually happens?

### Deep dive on a real missed opportunity - 20 minutes

1. Tell me about a relationship that went quiet, was lost, or took too long to resolve.
2. Reconstruct the timeline: prior calls, messages, commitments, objections, deadlines, and ownership changes.
3. What would a well-prepared caller have needed to know before the next call?
4. Which facts would be dangerous or inappropriate for an automated system to use or say?
5. What is the cost of not following up? How would you measure a better outcome?
6. What tools or people already attempt this? Where do they fail?
7. What calls are prohibited, require consent, require disclosure, or must always transfer to a human?
8. What would make you distrust a calling agent immediately?

### Concept reaction - 8 minutes

Read this neutrally:

> Imagine a system that keeps a limited, auditable timeline for each approved business relationship. It flags an overdue follow-up, prepares an approved call from relevant history, records the outcome, honors suppression rules, and hands exceptions to your team. It does not make commitments or take action outside its policy.

Then ask:

1. Where would that fit or fail in the workflow you described?
2. Which one call reason would you trust it with first?
3. What would it need to remember? What must it never retain?
4. What would you need to approve before a call can be made?
5. What result in 30 days would make this worth continuing?

### Wrap-up - 2 minutes

1. Who else should we speak with who sees this problem differently?
2. What question did we fail to ask?

## Interviewer rules

- Ask for concrete past behavior, not predictions about whether someone would use AI.
- Do not ask for names, phone numbers, recordings, account credentials, or customer data.
- Do not claim the system is legally compliant, autonomous, or ready to call anyone.
- Capture direct quotes separately from interpretation.
- Tag each observation as `workflow`, `context`, `outcome`, `authority`, `risk`, `tool-gap`, or `pilot-signal`.

## Synthesis card

Create one observation card per interview:

| Field | Capture |
| --- | --- |
| Segment / role | Who owns the process |
| Trigger | What made a call necessary |
| Current workaround | Person, CRM, spreadsheet, inbox, or nothing |
| Context required | Facts needed for a good next call |
| Failure mode | Why the call did not happen or went poorly |
| Consequence | Measurable loss, delay, or risk |
| Authority boundary | Consent, suppression, disclosure, transfer, and escalation needs |
| Pilot signal | Specific call reason and 30-day success metric |
| Direct quote | Verbatim evidence |

After six to eight interviews, affinity-map the cards by workflow and score each candidate using the first-wedge rule in [TotalRecall product discovery](totalrecall-product-discovery.md).
