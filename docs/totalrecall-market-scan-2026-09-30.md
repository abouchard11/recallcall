# TotalRecall market scan — 2026-09-30

## Decision this scan supports

This is a **research-prioritization memo**, not a market selection or a claim that no competitors exist. Its job is to keep TotalRecall away from the broad, crowded promise of “an AI agent that calls every lead” and direct the next six to eight interviews toward workflows where durable relationship context and an auditable handoff may still be the missing product.

## The product question

The customer-facing premise remains:

> TotalRecall is an AI calling agent that never forgets the relationship and never lets the next important call slip.

That only differentiates if the next call needs more than a campaign trigger and a generic script. A plausible first job needs all of these:

1. A delayed follow-up causes a visible business loss.
2. The correct next conversation depends on prior promises, stakeholders, objections, documents, or deadlines.
3. A human can configure who may be contacted, what may be said, and when the agent must hand off.
4. The outcome can be measured in a short, limited pilot.

## What the desk scan rules out as a default

### Contractor estimate recovery — crowded comparison category

This use case is real, but it is not an obvious first wedge. Open markets contractor voice automation around stale quote follow-up, and InvoicifyAI markets an estimate-follow-up voice agent that calls after a quote, captures commitments, and updates CRM records. That is direct evidence that the headline use case is already legible to vendors, not evidence that either product works or is a fit for a given buyer. [Open](https://www.open.cx/voice/for-contractors) · [InvoicifyAI](https://www.invoicifyai.com/estimate-followup-agent)

**Research use:** interview one or two commercial operators as a skeptical benchmark. Ask what a generic quote-follow-up agent misses: revision history, jobsite constraints, promised documents, decision committees, or exception ownership. Reject the segment if a cadence/voice product already handles the full job adequately.

### Staffing reactivation — specialized competition plus higher risk

Staffing vendors are already selling voice-led candidate reactivation and recruiter handoff. The category also raises more contact, employment, and candidate-trust concerns than an approved business-account follow-up workflow. [AI Job Agency](https://www.aijobagency.eu/blog/ai-voice-agent-candidate-reactivation-staffing-agencies/) · [VoiceDash](https://www.voice-dash.com/blog/voice-ai-for-staffing-agencies)

**Research use:** do not lead with this segment. Only interview it as a comparison if the operator can articulate a tightly approved, B2B client workflow with an accountable human owner.

## Interview hypotheses to test — not claims to sell

For every operator, start with a past, anonymized case. Do not pitch a solution until the current workflow is reconstructed.

| Hypothesis | Evidence that would support it | Evidence that would reject it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Memory, rather than call volume, is the core bottleneck | The caller must reconcile information across systems or people before safely calling, and the missing context changes the outcome. | A simple list of stale records plus a standard script produces the same result. |
| A governed calling agent can be trusted with one job | An operator can define a contact list, a single call reason, disclosure/suppression rules, escalation conditions, and a human owner. | The business cannot define a safe scope without broad autonomy, negotiation, or sensitive data access. |
| The gain is measurable in 30 days | The operator can name a baseline and target for completed follow-ups, qualified next steps, recovered pipeline, renewal handoffs, or avoided lapse. | The result is only general productivity enthusiasm or annual revenue attribution that cannot be observed during a pilot. |

## Research design

Use the [buyer interview guide](totalrecall-buyer-interview-guide.md) for six to eight 45-minute interviews over two weeks:

- two commercial-distribution / industrial-sales operators;
- two MSP renewal or dormant-account operators;
- two to four deliberately skeptical comparison interviews, starting with contractor estimate recovery and only then staffing if a responsible B2B context is available.

Record one synthesis card per interview. Then score each workflow against the first-wedge rule in [product discovery](totalrecall-product-discovery.md). Select nothing until at least three interviews in one workflow show weekly pain, relationship-specific context, a measurable loss, a permitted contact basis, an accountable operator, and a useful 30-day pilot.

## Product boundary maintained during research

The first pilot, if the interviews support one, is an approved B2B follow-up or explicitly requested callback. It is not cold consumer telemarketing, bulk robocalling, payment collection, negotiation, regulated advice, or unbounded access to CRM history. Covered telemarketing rules and the treatment of AI-generated voices make approval, suppression, calling windows, disclosure, records, and escalation product constraints rather than afterthoughts. This is product-design guidance, not legal advice. [FTC Telemarketing Sales Rule guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-telemarketing-sales-rule) · [FCC declaratory ruling on AI-generated voices](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-400393A1.pdf)
