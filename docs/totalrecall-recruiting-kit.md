# TotalRecall research recruiting kit

Use this material to recruit 6-8 discovery participants without promising a product, requesting customer data, or sending automated calls.

## Who to recruit first

Recruit two operators from each of three candidate workflows, plus one or two contrarian operators who use a different workaround:

- commercial distributor or industrial-sales teams that follow up on open quotes;
- managed-service providers with renewals or dormant opportunities; and
- commercial contractors or service businesses that recover bids, estimates, or missing documents.

Prioritize the person who actually owns the follow-up queue over an executive who only sees aggregate pipeline reports.

## Short outreach message

> Hi [Name] — I’m researching how teams keep important business follow-up calls from falling through the cracks. I’m not selling software or asking for customer data. I’d value 30-45 minutes to understand how your team follows up on [quotes / renewals / open work], what context callers need, and where the process breaks. In return, I’ll share the anonymized patterns I find. Would you be open to a short conversation next week?

## Warm-introduction request

> I’m interviewing operators who personally manage recurring business follow-up calls—especially open quotes, renewals, estimates, or stalled opportunities. The research is about the workflow, not a sales pitch, and I will not ask for customer data. Is there one person you trust who would be candid about where follow-up breaks down?

## Follow-up after a non-response

> Just closing the loop on this. I’m trying to understand one practical question: when a valuable relationship goes quiet, what information does a caller need to make the next call well? If a 30-minute conversation is not useful, no problem at all.

Send at most one follow-up. Log a declined invitation as a recruitment outcome and do not add it to any future outreach sequence without permission.

## Scheduling note

> This is a research conversation, not a product demonstration. Please do not prepare customer records, call recordings, credentials, or any personal contact information. A recent anonymized example of a stalled follow-up is enough.

## Screening checklist

Before scheduling, confirm that the participant can discuss:

- a recurring follow-up workflow that uses phone calls today;
- one recent anonymized relationship that stalled or required repeated follow-up;
- where the caller gets context and who owns the next step;
- what kind of calling is permitted or prohibited in their environment; and
- how they would measure a better result in a 30-day pilot.

## Research ethics and product boundaries

- Never represent TotalRecall as deployed, compliant in every jurisdiction, or ready to call a participant’s contacts.
- Never use recruitment to build a call list for a future automated campaign.
- Do not collect customer data, recordings, credentials, personal phone numbers, or regulated information.
- Respect a decline or opt-out immediately; the product must embody the follow-through and suppression rules it is studying.

## What counts as a useful signal

Strong signal is a concrete statement such as: “We lose track of open quotes every week, the next call depends on the history in three systems, and I would test an agent on this one approved list if it always handed exceptions back to my team.”

Weak signal is generic enthusiasm for AI, a request for a broad chatbot, or a workflow with no measurable loss, no calling authority, or no accountable owner.
